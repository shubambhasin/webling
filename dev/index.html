The quiet history of the internet's smallest residents

Long before anyone thought to put a creature on a web page, people were already decorating their corners of the internet with little things that moved: spinning globes, under-construction signs, cursors with comet tails.

This page is a test bed. Your webling should drop in from the top, walk along these lines of text, hop between paragraphs, and fall to the bottom of the screen when it runs out of road. Grab it and throw it.

Things it can stand on

Any line of visible text is a platform. Short lines, long lines, links like this one, and the headings above.

It ignores text in inputs, scripts and anything fixed to the screen, because those don't move with the page.

Things it eats

A cookie banner sits at the bottom of this page. A newsletter pop-up appears a few seconds after load. Watch your webling notice them, jump on, and chew its way across. Then press "Spit it out" to put them back.

Scroll down and it drops back in from the top. Leave the page alone for 45 seconds and it falls asleep.
